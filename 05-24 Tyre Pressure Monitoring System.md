# Tyre Pressure Monitoring System (TPMS) (if equipped)
The TPMS alerts you when one or more tyres is significantly under-inflated. Each wheel carries a sensor with a unique ID code that transmits tyre pressure data to the vehicle's body control module. When significant under-inflation is detected, the low tyre pressure warning light comes on.

> **WARNING:** TPMS is not a substitute for regular tyre maintenance. The warning light only activates when under-inflation is significant — moderately under-inflated tyres can still reduce load capacity, tyre life, and fuel efficiency without triggering the light. Check tyre pressure monthly when the tyres are cold regardless of whether the light is on.

> **WARNING:** The TPMS cannot alert you to tyre damage caused by external factors such as nails or road debris if no drop in inflation pressure is detected.

## Low Tyre Pressure Warning Light
Check each tyre monthly when cold and inflate to the pressure shown on the vehicle's tyre pressure placard or label.

**When the light comes on and stays on:** Reduce speed, avoid abrupt steering and braking, and stop in a safe place as soon as possible. Check all tyres. Driving on a significantly under-inflated tyre causes it to overheat and can lead to tyre failure, affecting steering control and braking effectiveness.
- If one or more tyres is flat, replace with the spare tyre (optional). Refer to [[10-05 Jacking Instructions]] in the EMERGENCY SERVICE section.
- If tyres are under-inflated, adjust all tyres to the recommended pressure as soon as possible.

**When the light does not come on for 2 seconds after ignition ON, or blinks while driving:** Have the vehicle inspected by a Maruti Suzuki authorised workshop. Even if the light stops blinking on its own, the system should still be checked.

> **WARNING:** Relying solely on TPMS to determine when to add air can result in loss of control or an accident. Always check pressures monthly with a gauge.

## Normal Causes for the Warning Light Coming On
The low tyre pressure warning light can come on due to normal causes unrelated to a puncture:
- Natural air leakage over time
- Pressure changes caused by temperature drops or changes in atmospheric pressure (e.g. at high altitude)

To turn off the light after adjusting pressures, inflate tyres to the correct cold pressure and drive for approximately 10 minutes. The light goes off when the adjusted pressure is recognised. If it comes back on shortly after, you may have a slow puncture.

## Temperature and Altitude Effects on Tyre Pressure
Tyre pressure changes with temperature. Always check and adjust pressure when tyres are cold:
- Tyres checked after driving will read higher than the actual cold pressure.
- Tyres inflated in a warm garage may fall below the specified pressure when driven in cold outdoor temperatures.
- If inflating in a garage that is warmer than the outside temperature, add 1 kPa to the recommended cold pressure for every 0.8°C difference between garage and outdoor temperature.
- At high altitude, the warning light may not turn off even after adjustment. In this case, inflate slightly above the placard pressure.

> **NOTE:** The light may turn off temporarily after coming on due to rising tyre temperature during extended driving or in a hot climate. Even if it turns off, check all tyre pressures.

## TPMS Malfunction Indicator
The malfunction indicator uses the same warning light as low tyre pressure. When a system malfunction is detected, the light flashes for approximately 75 seconds and then remains on continuously. This sequence repeats on each subsequent engine start until the fault is resolved.

While the malfunction indicator is on, the system may be unable to detect or signal low tyre pressure. Have the vehicle inspected by a Maruti Suzuki authorised workshop.

Common causes of TPMS malfunction include:
- Replacement or non-genuine tyres or wheels that prevent the sensors from communicating
- Damaged sensors from tyre installation or removal
- Use of liquid tyre sealant (not the repair kit supplied with the vehicle)

> **NOTICE:** Have tyres repaired or replaced by a Maruti Suzuki authorised workshop. Tyre pressure sensors can be damaged during tyre installation or removal by non-authorised workshops.

> **NOTICE:** If the warning light comes on frequently, there may be a problem with one or more tyres, sensors, or the monitoring system. Have the vehicle inspected.

## TPMS Limitations
The warning light may come on and stay on, or blink, in the following situations:
- When a flat tyre has been replaced with the spare (the spare does not have a TPMS sensor)
- When the spare tyre is included in a tyre rotation
- When a TPMS sensor is damaged during tyre replacement or liquid sealant is used
- When radio frequency interference is present (from nearby electronic devices, metallic window film, heavy snow or ice around the wheels, or non-genuine snow tyres without TPMS sensors)
- When non-genuine Maruti Suzuki wheels or tyres are used
- When any tyre pressure is too high
- When there is a problem with the TPMS controller

## Checking Current Tyre Pressure on the Information Display
The display may not show tyre pressures for the first approximately 10 minutes of driving while the system reads the sensors. If the vehicle has been parked with the ignition off for 20 minutes or more, pressures reset to "--- kPa" and recover after driving for a while. If a low-pressure tyre was detected before parking, the reading may remain as "---" until the issue is resolved.

When the warning light comes on, the display automatically switches to the tyre pressure view and the affected tyre(s) blink.

### Instrument Cluster Type A
1. With the ignition ON and the vehicle stationary, press and hold the indicator selector knob for more than 3 seconds to enter setting mode.
2. Turn the knob to select "TPMS" and press to confirm.
3. Turn the knob to select "Tyre Press" and press to confirm.
4. Current tyre pressures are displayed.

To exit setting mode, select "Back" and press the knob.

### Instrument Cluster Type B
1. With the ignition ON and the vehicle stationary, press the left/right selector switch to enter setting mode.
2. Select "Vehicle settings" and press the OK switch.
3. Select "TPMS" and press the OK switch.
4. Select "Tyre pressure" and press the OK switch.
5. Current tyre pressures are displayed.

To exit setting mode, press the left/right selector switch.

## Replacing Tyres and/or Wheels
**After a flat tyre:** If replaced with the spare (optional), have a Maruti Suzuki authorised workshop mount a new tyre on the original wheel. The TPMS is already programmed with the original wheel's sensor ID and normal operation will be restored automatically.

**When replacing any wheel:** A Maruti Suzuki authorised workshop must install TPMS sensors in the new wheels and register the new sensor IDs with the system.

**Spare tyre:** The spare tyre does not have a TPMS sensor. Use it only in emergencies and replace it with a standard tyre as soon as possible to restore normal TPMS operation.

> **WARNING:** Use of tyres or wheels not recommended by Maruti Suzuki can cause TPMS failure. Use only manufacturer-recommended tyres and wheels. Refer to [[09-13 Tyres]] in the INSPECTION AND MAINTENANCE section.

> **WARNING:** Tampering with, modifying, or disabling TPMS components may prevent the system from warning you of low tyre pressure or malfunctions, and may void the warranty for that portion of the vehicle.
